# Free, Machine-Readable Country-Level Data Sources — Sourcing Inventory

**Purpose.** Inventory of freely accessible, machine-readable sources for country-level macro, demographic,
governance, climate, infrastructure and long-run financial-return indicators, for a Python/dbt platform that must
be able to rebuild the dataset legally and for free (open licence), ideally via API or bulk download.

**Verification method.** Every URL marked ✅ was HTTP-probed live from this machine with `curl`
(status code + byte count recorded). URLs marked ⚠️ were found but return 403/429 to `curl` (bot wall,
rate limit) or are documented-but-unprobed. URLs marked ❌ are confirmed dead/invalid — do not use.
"Bare-curl 403" is usually Cloudflare/bot filtering: add a browser `User-Agent`, or better, hit the
machine endpoint (SDMX/file path) instead of the HTML landing page.

Conventions used below: `{ISO3}` = ISO-3166 alpha-3, `{ISO2}`, `{FLOW}` = SDMX dataflow id.

## Traps and gaps

Where free data **does not exist** and a proxy or a paid/two-tier source is unavoidable.

**1. Cost of living — no free redistributable source.**
Numbeo is proprietary, crowd-sourced, and its terms **forbid republication**; the free API tier is unusable for a
redistributable product. Mercer and EIU cost-of-living data are outright paid. **Proxy:** World Bank ICP
`PA.NUS.PPP` ÷ `PA.NUS.FCRF` gives a price-level index that is CC BY 4.0, keyless and covers ~180 economies — but it is
a **3-yearly ICP round**, not a monthly city index. Watch out: the legacy code **`PA.NUS.PPPC.RF` returns an empty
payload with HTTP 200** — a silent failure.

**2. Liveability / quality-of-life rankings — paywalled, full stop.**
Mercer Quality of Living and the EIU Global Liveability Index have **no free machine-readable version**; EIU gives
only a lead-captured summary article. **Proxy:** UNDP HDI, WHO HALE, OECD Better Life Index (SDMX), and the free
World Happiness Report data appendix.

**3. Sovereign credit ratings — no free feed, and the obvious workaround is dead.**
The **Trading Economics guest API now returns `410 "guest account has been discontinued"`**. No agency publishes a
usable RSS (Fitch/Moody's `/rss` serve HTML app shells with status 200 — soft-404s; S&P 403s). JPMorgan EMBI is paid
and **FRED has no EMBI or EM sovereign spread series at all**. The only free routes are **HTML scrapes** of
`tradingeconomics.com/country-list/rating` or `worldgovernmentbonds.com/world-credit-ratings/` (robots.txt is
permissive), or a stale GitHub dataset. **Better plan: build a market-implied proxy** — BIS `WS_CBPOL` plus
`IRLTLT01{ISO3}M156N` yields spread over US Treasuries, combined with World Bank CPIA (`IQ.CPA.DEBT.XQ`) and
external-debt/reserves ratios. Accept that you will not have a letter rating for free.

**4. Long-run asset returns — free data is thin and non-commercial.**
The **UBS/CS Global Investment Returns Yearbook country series is not free** — the landing page states the full book
requires a **UBS Investment Bank client login** on UBS Neo, with no retail price, and the underlying Dimson-Marsh-Staunton
database is not sold standalone. JST R6 is free but **CC BY-NC-SA (non-commercial)**, covers only **18 advanced economies**,
and has **no `rgdppc` and no `mortg`** variable. Shiller is **US-only with no licence grant**. Damodaran has **no
international annual-returns file** — the `histgr*` files are *growth* rates, and country coverage comes only as equity
risk premia (`ctryprem.xlsx`). Ken French gives **factors, not country total returns**. Barclays Equity-Gilt Study is
**not purchasable** at all. **Net: for global long-run returns you either accept 18 countries non-commercially (JST)
or pay LSEG/GFD.**

**5. Governance — three of the most-cited indices are non-commercial or permission-gated.**
**Freedom House** (FIW, FOTN): no machine-readable file at all, HTML scrape only, and **commercial use requires prior
permission**. **Global Peace Index**: CC BY-NC-SA, form-gated, with a mandatory **USD $2,000–$4,000 commercial licence**.
**Fragile States Index**: went **PDF-only after 2023**; republishing needs permission. **Polity5 is frozen at 2018** and
prohibits redistribution; **CIRI is discontinued**; the **EIU Democracy Index is paid**. Only **WGI (CC BY 4.0)**, **WJP**
(free, no registration) and **V-Dem (CC BY-SA)** are genuinely usable — and V-Dem is viral and version-locked.

**6. Broadband performance — non-commercial licence.**
Ookla Open Data is **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, which bars commercial use; the free Speedtest Global Index has **no API and no
bulk download**. Cloudflare Radar is free but **restricts redistribution** and is a sample of Cloudflare traffic, not a
census. There is **no free, commercially licensable, measured broadband dataset**.

**7. ITU / telecoms — portal-walled.**
`datahub.itu.int` **403s automated access**, and there is **no public ITU API**. Only the regional/global aggregate XLSX
is reliably fetchable; country-level ICT detail effectively requires a manual DataHub export. Same story for **IEA**,
where only headline aggregates are free and the full energy balances/CO₂ databases are paid.

**8. Data-centre capacity — does not exist free.**
No vendor publishes an official machine-readable region list with coordinates, and there is **no free global
data-centre capacity or utilisation dataset** at all. You get region codes and IP prefixes (AWS `ip-ranges.json`),
plus community MIT-licensed lists that go stale. Capacity/occupancy is a paid-market-research product.

**9. Air quality — key-gated and biased.**
**OpenAQ v2 is retired (`410 Gone`)** and **v3 requires a key (401 keyless)**; the WHO database is **city-level only**
and its direct `.xlsx` URL 404s. Station density is heavily skewed to Europe and North America, so country means are
biased.

**10. Passport / visa-free indices — all three brands ban scraping.**
Henley's ToS explicitly prohibits *"any robot, spider, scraper, or other automated means"*; Arton Capital 403s behind a
Cloudflare challenge; VisaIndex bans copying. **Use the MIT-licensed `imorte/passport-index-data` CSV** — but note it
and its siblings all derive from passportindex.org, so they are **not independent** and cannot be cross-validated
against each other. DEMIG VISA is free but **ends in 2013**.

**11. Silent-failure traps that pass naive status-code checks.**
- 🚨 **UNESCO UIS silently ignores the `indicators=` parameter** — `CR.1`, `LR.AG15T99` and `ZZZ.BOGUS.999` return the **byte-identical 4,205,172-byte payload** at HTTP 200 (verified by A/B test). A pipeline will ingest the wrong indicator and never error. The UIS API must be treated as untrusted until this is fixed.
- **FRED ICE BofA OAS series** return **HTTP 200 with only a rolling ~3-year window** even when you pass `cosd=1990-01-01` — licence-driven truncation.
- **World Bank `PA.NUS.PPPC.RF`** (404/empty) and **`CC.EST`** (128-byte empty payload) return HTTP 200 on failure.
- **Stooq CSV** (`stooq.com/q/d/l/?s=^spx&i=d`) returns **HTTP 200 but a JavaScript proof-of-work challenge**, not CSV.
- **US Census API** keyless calls return **HTTP 200 with HTML "Missing Key"**.
- **WHO `HRH_26`** returns **HTTP 200 with 0 rows** — an empty-but-valid response that looks like success.
- **Fitch/Moody's `/rss`** return **HTTP 200 `text/html` app shells** (soft-404s) — no ratings feed.
- **Henley's SPA returns HTTP 200 for every path**, so status proves nothing; its `robots.txt` is not a robots.txt.
- **Bare-curl 403** from Cloudflare on UN DESA, OECD, WHO, World Bank (all of `worldbank.org`), ITU and IMF landing pages — always send a browser `User-Agent` and prefer machine endpoints over HTML pages. **The World Bank CCKP is the worst case: every path, including the homepage, returns `403` with a `cf-mitigated: challenge` header, and a browser UA does not help — it needs a headless browser.**
- **BIS/OECD SDMX: a wrong dimension returns `NoResultsFound`, not an error** — e.g. OECD methodology must be `N`, not `_Z`; BIS country keys are ISO-2.

**13. Vintage / revision handling — the systemic gap.**
Almost none of these sources expose a vintage column. **WGI rewrote all history back to 1996 in the 2025 revision**;
**V-Dem re-runs its Bayesian IRT model every March and explicitly warns that index values are not comparable across
versions**; **CPI is not comparable before 2012**; **EPI and RSF break methodology every edition**; **WEO vintages are
overwritten each round**; **INFORM and BTI break between editions**; **Ember's bulk CSV is re-issued in place with no
history**. The platform must **snapshot every pull with an `as_of_date` and never mix vintages in one series.**

**14. URL rot is the norm, not the exception.**
Sources whose download URL **changes on every release** and therefore must be re-scraped rather than hard-coded:
ND-GAIN (`/assets/{numeric-id}/`), EPI (**dated filenames are mandatory** — `epi2026results.xlsx` alone 404s),
INFORM Risk (`_v072` → `_v073`), NOAA NCEI (`…v6.0.0.202512.asc`), WDPA (`current/WDPA_Sep2026_…`), Hansen
(`GFC-2025-v1.13/` ×2 in the path), CRU TS, HadCRUT5, UN WPP (`WPP2024_…`), UNDP HDR (`2025_HDR/HDR25_…`),
FAOSTAT (`(Normalized)` filenames), WHO AAQD (`?sfvrsn=` token), Chinn-Ito (`kaopen_2023.xls`), IMF WEO bulk,
and the V-Dem edition pages. **Build a "resolve latest URL" step per source with a parser + assertion on the
expected file extension, rather than storing the URL.**

### Design rules that fall out of the above
1. **Every table carries `as_of_date` (pull date) + `source_vintage`.** No exceptions — WGI, V-Dem, WEO, CPI, EPI and RSF all break comparability across releases.
2. **Separate "fetch" from "publish".** Fetch daily/weekly into an immutable raw landing zone; publish only on a green data-quality gate (row counts, country counts, freshness).
3. **Route every source through one HTTP client** with a browser `User-Agent`, per-host token-bucket rate limits (IMF and OECD both 429/403 on bursts), exponential backoff, and a response-content check — **assert on content type and body shape, never on status code alone** (Stooq, Census, FRED-OAS and WB `PA.NUS.PPPC.RF` all return 200 on failure).
4. **Licence-tier the warehouse.** Tag every source `open` / `nc` / `restricted` / `paid`. Ookla (NC-SA), WHO (NC-SA), JST (NC-SA), ND-GAIN, GPI (NC-SA), Protected Planet, Freedom House, FSI, GBD (NC) and TeleGeography cannot ship in a commercial product without a licence; WDI, WGI, Eurostat, FAOSTAT, V-Dem, EPI, Ember, OONI, UIS and open-meteo can.
5. **Prefer bulk over API for annual sources, API over bulk for monthly.** WDI bulk (283 MB, one file) beats 1,400 paginated calls; FRED/ECB/BIS per-series CSV beats a 300 MB dump.
6. **Cache dataflow/structure discovery for 30 days.** The structure endpoints (OECD dataflows, BIS dataflow, Eurostat dataflow at 37 MB) are the expensive calls and change rarely.
7. **Add a per-source "resolve latest URL" step.** Fourteen of these sources publish under a URL that changes every release (see Traps §14). Store a resolver + a content assertion, never a frozen URL.
8. **Prefer keyless bulk over key-gated APIs where a licence allows.** Concretely: FAOSTAT bulk ZIPs over the tokenised API; Hansen GCS GeoTIFFs over the GFW API; Ember's GCS bulk CSV over its keyed API; WDPA's S3 bucket over its token API; UN WPP bulk CSV over its 401-ing Data Portal API; FRED `fredgraph.csv` over the keyed FRED API.
