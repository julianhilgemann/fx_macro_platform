## 2. Capital gains — STATUS OF THE 66.67% PROPOSAL

**CURRENT LAW: the inclusion rate is 50% for individuals. VERIFIED on the CRA form actually used for 2025 returns.**
- CRA *Schedule 3, Capital Gains or Losses* (2025): line reads "**Inclusion rate × 50%**". https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/5000-s3/5000-s3-25e.pdf

**The increase did NOT proceed — authoritative sources:**
- **Department of Finance deferral:** announced **31 January 2025** by Minister of Finance and Intergovernmental Affairs Dominic LeBlanc — the change (originally for gains realized after 25 June 2024) was **deferred to 1 January 2026**. https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en-ca/expert-insights/changes-to-capital-gains-inclusion-rate-deferred-to-2026
- **Revenu Québec harmonisation with the deferral to 1 January 2026**, news release **5 February 2025**: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/fr/salle-de-presse/nouvelles-fiscales/details/2025-02-05/harmonisation-avec-le-report-au-1er-janvier-2026-de-la-mise-en-oeuvre-du-changement-du-taux-dinclusion-des-gains-en-capital/
- **Cancellation — Prime Minister of Canada news release, 21 March 2025:** "the Government of Canada will cancel the proposed hike in the capital gains inclusion rate." Same release confirms the **Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption limit stays at $1,250,000** for small business shares and farming/fishing property. https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2025/03/21/prime-minister-mark-carney-cancels-proposed-capital-gains-tax-increase
- **PwC *Tax Facts and Figures: Canada 2025*** (footnote repeated per province): "Changes to the capital gains inclusion rate, which were to be effective for capital gains realized after June 24, 2024, **will no longer proceed**." https://www.pwc.com/ca/en/tax/publications/tax-facts-figures-2025-en.pdf
- **No 2026 reinstatement:** the **Spring Economic Update 2026** tax-measures annex (page date 2026-04-28) contains **no capital-gains measure** — only DTC, Employee Ownership Trust, Home Buyers' Plan, Labour Mobility Deduction, LNG CCA and CCUS/EOR measures. https://budget.canada.ca/update-miseajour/2026/report-rapport/tm-mf-en.html

**Critical legal nuance (UNVERIFIED as a formal statement):** the 66.67% / $250,000-threshold proposal was released as a **Notice of Ways and Means Motion (June 2024)** and, per the sources above, was never enacted; the implementing bill died before enactment. I could **not** find a dedicated statute repealing it, because it never became law. The operative verification is that (a) the government announced cancellation, and (b) CRA's 2025 Schedule 3 still applies a flat 50%. ✅ 50% is the law for 2025 and 2026.

**CRA's administrative position during the deferral:** CRA administered the **50% rate as the law in force**, with the 66.67% change held over to 1 January 2026 (Finance deferral + Revenu Québec harmonisation above). **UNVERIFIED:** the specific interim CRA forms/administrative guidance issued during the deferral window.

**$250,000 threshold detail:** the threshold applied only under the (abandoned) 2024 proposal, i.e. 66.67% inclusion on gains **above $250,000** for individuals. Because the proposal was cancelled, **no $250,000 threshold applies** — 50% applies to the entire gain. ✅

**Top marginal EFFECTIVE capital gains rate (= 50% × top combined rate):**
- **Ontario: 26.77%** (2025 at ≥$253,414; 2026 at ≥$258,482)
- **Alberta: 24.00%** (2025 at ≥$362,961; 2026 at ≥$370,220)
- Québec 26.66%; BC 26.75% — BDO *Tax Facts 2025* and *Tax Facts 2026* (URLs in §1).

**Principal residence exemption:** **UNVERIFIED** — the CRA principal-residence page returned a 404 and I did not obtain the s.40(2) formula, the "+1 year" rule, or the PRE-at-death treatment.

## 4. Net wealth tax and estate/inheritance tax

- **No estate/inheritance tax; deemed disposition at death:** the ITA instead deems a disposition at fair market value immediately before death (ITA s.70). **UNVERIFIED** — I located references to ITA s.70(6) and s.70(9.2) but did not fetch the section text, so I cannot certify the precise wording or subsection.
- **Graduated rate estate:** the term is **verified as existing in the ITA** — s.220(4.51) refers to "a graduated rate estate or a qualified disability trust as defined in subsection 122(3)." The **36-month** duration rule is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Spousal rollover:** **UNVERIFIED** (only a Tax Interpretations roundtable title referencing s.70(6) and testamentary spousal trusts was seen).
- **Probate / estate administration tax — Ontario (VERIFIED):** https://www.ontario.ca/page/estate-administration-tax
  - **$0 tax if estate value ≤ $50,000** (Estate Information Return still required within 180 days of the estate certificate).
  - Over $50,000: **$15 per $1,000 (or part thereof)** of estate value; value rounded up to the nearest $1,000. **$0 per $1,000 on the first $50,000.** Worked example on the page: $240,000 estate → $190 × $15 = **$2,850**.
  - **Pre-1 January 2020** rates: **$5 per $1,000** on the first $50,000 + **$15 per $1,000** above $50,000.
  - Most assets included (real estate in Ontario net of encumbrances, bank accounts incl. foreign, investments incl. TFSA/RRSP/RRIF, vehicles/vessels, all other property wherever situated, insurance payable to the estate); excluded: real estate outside Ontario, jointly-held assets passing by survivorship, named-beneficiary RRSP/RRIF/TFSA/RPP, CPP death benefit.

## 5. Pre-existing foreign capital for a NEW resident; residency rules

**Statutory text VERIFIED — ITA s.250** (Act current to 2026-07-21; last amended 2026-06-18): https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-250.html
- **s.250(1)(a) — the "sojourner" rule:** a person is **deemed resident in Canada throughout a taxation year if the person "sojourned in Canada in the year for a period of, or periods the total of which is, 183 days or more."**
- **s.250(3):** a reference to a person resident in Canada includes a person **ordinarily resident** in Canada (factual residence).
- **s.250(5) — deemed non-resident / treaty tie-breaker:** "a person is deemed not to be resident in Canada at a time if, at that time, the person would, but for this subsection and any tax treaty, be resident in Canada for the purposes of this Act but is, under a tax treaty with another country, resident in the other country and not resident in Canada." (Overrides all other ITA provisions except s.126(1.1)(a).)
- Other deemed-resident categories in s.250(1): Canadian Forces members (b); ambassadors/officers/servants of Canada or a province (c); prescribed international development assistance programs (d); overseas Canadian Forces school staff (d.1); dependant children (f); certain treaty-exempt family members (g).

**CRA operational guidance (VERIFIED):** residency is determined on **all relevant facts**, including residential ties with Canada and "the length of time, purpose, intent and continuity of the stay." The CRA decision tree uses the **183-day** test plus whether the individual has established ties in a treaty country and is treated as resident there, and whether they are ordinarily resident in Canada. Forms: **NR73** (opinion on residency status) and **NR74** (opinion on whether you are a deemed non-resident on entering Canada). CRA folio **S5-F1-C1, Determining an Individual's Residence Status**. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/determining-your-residency-status.html

**Worldwide taxation from day one for residents:** **UNVERIFIED** as an explicit citation (rests on ITA s.3, which I did not fetch). The CRA T4058 guide refers to "net world income … the total of net income from all sources both inside and outside of Canada for the period you were a resident."

**Treatment of pre-existing foreign capital on becoming resident — VERIFIED from ITA s.128.1(1)(b):** an individual who becomes resident is deemed to dispose of each property immediately before becoming resident at FMV and to reacquire it at that cost (s.128.1(1)(c)). For an **individual**, the excluded categories are: (i) **taxable Canadian property**, (ii) inventory of a business carried on in Canada, (iii) Class 14.1 property of a business carried on in Canada, and (iv) an **excluded right or interest**. Consequence: **non-excluded foreign property (e.g. foreign shares) is stepped up to FMV on immigration**, so pre-residency appreciation is not taxed in Canada. https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-128.1.html

## 7. Exit / departure tax

**Statutory text VERIFIED — ITA s.128.1(4)(b) and s.220** (ITA current to 2026-07-21): https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-128.1.html · https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-220.html

- **s.128.1(4)(b) — deemed disposition on ceasing residence:** the taxpayer is deemed to dispose, immediately before ceasing to be resident, of **each property owned**, at **fair market value**, for individuals **except**: (i) real/immovable property situated in Canada, Canadian resource property, timber resource property; (ii) capital property used in, Class 14.1 property of, or inventory of a business carried on **through a permanent establishment in Canada**; (iii) an **excluded right or interest**; (iv) for a person not resident in Canada for **more than 60 of the preceding 120 months**, property owned when they last became resident or acquired by inheritance/bequest; (v) property elected under s.128.1(6)(a).
- **s.128.1(4)(c):** deemed reacquisition at those proceeds (new cost base).
- **s.128.1(4)(d):** **elective** disposition for property in (b)(i)/(ii) (Canadian real property and Canadian business property) — lets the departing individual bump these too.
- **s.128.1(5) — instalment relief:** for an individual deemed to have disposed of property, instalment interest under ss.155/156/156.1/161(2),(4),(4.01) is computed **as if the departure-tax deemed disposition did not apply**.
- **s.128.1(6) — returning former resident:** elections available, and (6)(c) lets the individual reduce proceeds/cost by the lesser of the s.128.1(4) gain, FMV at re-entry, and an amount specified.

**Deferral / security — VERIFIED:**
- **s.220(4):** the Minister may accept **security** for any amount payable under the Act.
- **s.220(4.5) "Security for departure tax":** an individual deemed by s.128.1(4) to have disposed of property (other than an employee benefit plan right/interest) may **elect, in prescribed manner, on or before the balance-due day for the emigration year**; the Minister **shall accept adequate security** until the balance-due day of a later year, for the formula amount A − B − [((A − B)/A) × C]. While security is accepted, **interest and penalties are computed as if the secured amount had been paid** (except ss.161(2),(4),(4.01)).
- **s.220(4.51) "Deemed security":** on making the election the Minister is **deemed** to have accepted adequate security for the **lesser of** (a) the tax a **Canadian-resident trust with $50,000 taxable income** would pay for the year, and (b) the greatest amount for which security is required. **This is the mechanism that gives most emigrants an automatic small deferral.**
- **s.220(4.52):** cap on deemed security; **s.220(4.53):** inadequate-security top-up within 90 days; **s.220(4.54):** extensions; **s.220(4.7):** **undue hardship** — the Minister may accept security of lesser value.
- **"$1,000,000 deferral": UNVERIFIED — and appears incorrect.** The current s.220(4.5)/(4.51) text contains **no $1,000,000 amount**; the deemed-security measure is tied to the tax of a trust with $50,000 taxable income. I found no source supporting a $1,000,000 departure-tax deferral.
- **"Departure tax deferral for instalments":** the verified provision is **s.128.1(5)** (instalment-interest computation relief), not a separate deferral programme.

**Forms and reporting — partially verified:**
- **Form T1161, *List of Properties by an Emigrant of Canada*** — required if **the total FMV of all property owned when leaving Canada was more than $25,000** (CRA, *Leaving Canada (emigrants)*): https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/leaving-canada-emigrants.html

**Non-resident withholding — VERIFIED:** CRA *T4058, Non-Residents and Income Tax — 2025*: Canadian payers "**withhold non-resident tax at a rate of 25%** on certain types of Canadian-source income," and the withheld non-resident tax is generally the **final tax**. The guide also states a buyer may be entitled to withhold or recover **25% (50% on certain types of property)** of the cost of taxable Canadian property acquired. https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pub/t4058/t4058-25e.pdf

## 8. Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT)

**VERIFIED from the CRA AMT form itself (T691):**
- **Rate: 20.5%** — T691 2025, line 96 ("Federal tax rate × 20.5%"); also line in Part 7 at "Applicable rate × 20.5%". Same 20.5% on T691 2024. https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/t691/t691-25e.pdf
- **Basic exemption:** **$173,205 for 2024** (T691 2024, line 95) and **$177,882 for 2025** (T691 2025, line 94). https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/t691/t691-24e.pdf · https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/t691/t691-25e.pdf
- **2026 exemption = $181,440** ✅ (CRA indexed-amounts page shows $177,882 and $181,440 among the indexed figures: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/frequently-asked-questions-individuals/adjustment-personal-income-tax-benefit-amounts.html ; also independently confirmed by you). Note $181,440 is also the 2026 top of the 26% bracket, consistent with the exemption being tied to that bracket threshold.
- **Effectively 100% capital gains inclusion for AMT — VERIFIED:** T691 2025 Part 3 adds back the "**Non-taxable part of capital gains reported in the year — amount from line 19700 of Schedule 3**" (line 15), i.e. the 50% otherwise-excluded half is added back, producing 100% inclusion for AMT.
- **Other AMT add-backs/rates on the 2025 form:** capital gains on gifts of publicly listed securities × **30%** (line 26, from Form T1170); lifetime capital gains exemption × **40%** (line 87); resource property/flow-through shares (line 14); certain capital gains of graduated rate estates; amount B = C × **27.5362%** for the security-options deduction adjustment.
- **Carryover:** T691 has **Part 8 — Applying a minimum tax carryover from previous years against 2025 tax payable** (line 12 entered on line 40427), and Part 7 accumulates "Additional 2025 taxes for carryover to later years" (line 14). **The number of carryforward years (the "new 30% AMT credit carryforward rules" / 7-year period) is UNVERIFIED** — the form does not state the period on its face.
- **Scope:** "Alternative minimum tax does not apply to a person who died in 2025 or to returns filed under subsections 70(2) or 150(4), or under paragraphs 104(23)(d) or 128(2)(e) of the ITA" (T691 2025).
- **Enacting legislation — PARTIALLY VERIFIED:** the ITA's Minimum Tax division is **s.127.5** (T691 cites **s.127.52**), and ITA amendment histories I read reference **"2024, c. 17"** (e.g. s.250 history: "2024, c. 17, s. 76"). **UNVERIFIED:** that S.C. 2024, c. 17 *is* Bill C-59; I did not reach the statute's own page (the KPMG Bill C-59 note URL returned HTML/404).
- **Effective date of the AMT reform: 2024 taxation year** — confirmed by the 20.5% rate and $173,205 exemption appearing on the **2024** T691.
