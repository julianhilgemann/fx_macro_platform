## 2. CAPITAL GAINS TAX — LISTED SECURITIES AND OTHER ASSETS (resident individual)

- **No separate capital gains tax**: gains form part of the aggregate PIT base at 15%/23% — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/income-determination ; ZDP § 16 (https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586)
- **Holding-period ("time test") exemptions, ZDP § 4 (current consolidated text, verified Sept 2026):**
  - **Securities: >3 years** between acquisition and sale — § 4(1)(w): "přesáhne-li doba mezi nabytím a úplatným převodem tohoto cenného papíru … dobu 3 let"; **kmenový list (profit participation certificate): 5 years** — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586 ; PwC: "Three years for direct ownership of securities (such as shares of a joint stock company)" — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/income-determination
  - **Share in a company (podíl v obchodní korporaci): >5 years** — § 4(1)(u): "přesahuje-li doba mezi jeho nabytím a úplatným převodem dobu 5 let"; PwC: "five years for shares in other companies not represented by a security" — same URLs
  - **Immovable property: >10 years** for property acquired from 2021 (§ 4(1)(b): "přesáhne-li doba mezi nabytím vlastnického práva … dobu 10 let"); **5 years for real estate purchased up to 2020** (PwC: "five years for real estate in other cases - purchased until 2020") — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586 | https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/income-determination . **⚠ Correction to prompt:** the current statutory test is 10 years (not 5), and the "10 years if acquired before 2011" formulation is not the rule in force.
  - **Own dwelling used for housing: 2 years** immediately before sale (or shorter if proceeds used for own housing needs) — § 4(1)(a) — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586
  - **Cars, aircraft, vessels: 1 year** — § 4 (same URL); PwC: "One year for cars, ships, and planes."
  - **Business property (obchodní majetek) assets are carved out** of the exemptions; a **3-year tail for securities / 5-year tail for corporate shares** applies after termination of self-employment activity — § 4(1)(w) and § 4(1)(u) (same URL)
  - **Immovable property: proceeds used for own housing needs** also exempts a sale within 10 years — § 4(1)(b) (same URL)

## 3. DIVIDEND TAXATION (resident individual)

- **Czech-source dividends paid by a Czech company to a Czech-resident individual: FINAL withholding tax of 15%** — ZDP § 36(2): "Zvláštní sazba daně … pro poplatníky uvedené v § 2 a 17 … činí 15 %, a to a) z podílu na zisku z účasti na obchodní společnosti … b) z podílu na zisku z účasti na společnosti s ručením omezeným, z účasti komanditisty … c) … z členství v družstvu" — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586 ; PwC: "Dividends … paid by a Czech resident entity to a Czech tax resident are all subject to WHT of 15%" — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/income-determination . **15% applies in both 2025 and 2026; no change enacted.** Because it is a final special-rate tax, such dividends are **not** included in the return and are **not** subject to 23%.
- **Non-resident rates:** 15% for residents of EU/EEA or treaty/exchange-of-information states; **35%** otherwise (ZDP § 36(1)(c); PwC) — same URLs.
- **15%/23% treatment when dividends are included in the return:** this happens for **foreign-source** dividends. Two options: (a) include in the general base → progressive 15%/23% (with treaty/§ 38f foreign tax credit); or (b) **elect the separate tax base under ZDP § 16a(1) taxed at a flat 15%**, but no allowances/deductions/credits can be applied against it — PwC: "A special tax base with the rate of 15% applies to selected types of non-Czech investment income (e.g. dividends and interest from bonds from abroad). Individuals can choose to include capital income from abroad in this separate tax base … However, tax allowances or tax-deductible items cannot be applied" — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; § 16a(1): "Sazba daně pro samostatný základ daně činí 15 %" — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586
- **No participation exemption for individuals.** The exemption for dividends from EU/EEA/Swiss subsidiaries (ZDP § 19(1)(ze)/(zi), § 19(9)–(11)) is drafted for **corporate** taxpayers ("obchodní korporace"); a Czech-resident individual receiving a dividend bears the 15% final WHT regardless of the profits having already been taxed at company level — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586
- **Effective combined top rate:** Czech-source dividends = **15% final** (= 15% total, no surcharge); foreign-source dividends routed through the return = up to **23%** (plus possible foreign WHT, relieved by credit) or a flat **15%** in the elective separate base — sources above.

## 4. ANNUAL NET WEALTH TAX / INHERITANCE / GIFTS

- **No annual net wealth tax** — PwC: "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in the Czech Republic." — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/other-taxes . (Only an annual **real-estate property tax** on real estate held at 1 January — same URL.)
- **No separate inheritance tax and no separate gift tax: abolished with effect from 1 January 2014** (Senate measure No. 344/2013 Coll.) and replaced by income-tax treatment under ZDP § 4a and § 10 — act record: https://psp.cz/sqw/sbirka.sqw?cz=344&r=2013 ; PwC: "There is no inheritance tax. Gifts are not taxed by a special gift tax, but rather they are included in the recipient's PIT. A tax exemption applies to gifts received from close relatives." — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/other-taxes
- **Inheritance: EXEMPT for ALL beneficiaries (no group test).** ZDP § 4a: "Od daně z příjmů fyzických osob se osvobozuje bezúplatný příjem a) z nabytí dědictví nebo odkazu" — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586 . **⚠ Correction to prompt:** the "exempt only for the first three groups, taxable at 15% for others" model is the pre-2014 inheritance-tax regime; it is **not** current law. (What remains taxable is *income generated by* inherited assets — e.g. § 10(1)(d) income from inherited IP rights, or a later sale of inherited property, with the deceased's holding period credited for the time tests.)
- **Gifts: exemption depends on the donor.** Exempt under ZDP § 10(3)(c) if received from: (1) a relative in the direct line, and in the collateral line if a **sibling, uncle, aunt, nephew or niece**, spouse, child's spouse, spouse's child, spouse's parents, or the spouse of one's parents; (2) a person with whom the recipient lived in a common household for ≥1 year immediately before the gift caring for the household or being dependent on them; (3) the settlor of a trust/contribution to a family foundation; **(5) "occasional" gifts where the aggregate from the same person in the tax year does not exceed CZK 50,000** — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586
  - **Nephews/nieces ARE in the exempt list** ("synovec nebo neteř") — same URL.
  - **Non-exempt donors (e.g. a friend, or a relative outside the list):** gifts up to **CZK 50,000 per donor per tax year** are exempt; the excess is taxable as "other income" (§ 10) in the **general base → 15%, and 23% on the part of the base above the 36× average-wage threshold** (§ 16) — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586 . No separate 15% flat rate applies; reporting is via the annual return, which must be filed if annual taxable income exceeds CZK 50,000 (§ 38g(1), same URL).
- **No exit/wealth-related annual levy** exists in Czech law (see § 7 below).

## 5. PRE-EXISTING (FOREIGN-SOURCE) CAPITAL FOR A NEW RESIDENT

- **Worldwide taxation of residents:** ZDP § 2(2): "Daňoví rezidenti České republiky mají daňovou povinnost, která se vztahuje jak na příjmy plynoucí ze zdrojů na území České republiky, tak i na příjmy plynoucí ze zdrojů v zahraničí." Non-residents only on Czech-source income (§ 2(3), § 22) — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586 ; PwC: "Czech tax residents are generally subject to Czech income tax on their worldwide income." — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
- **NO remittance basis and NO non-dom regime:** no such provisions exist in the ZDP; the only carve-outs are categorical (e.g. study/treatment stays are non-resident, § 2(3); foreign PE income exempted under treaty method rules, § 38f) — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586
- **NO annual wealth tax → pre-existing foreign capital is not taxed annually** — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/other-taxes
- **NO step-up in cost base on becoming resident.** ZDP § 10(5): for assets under § 10(1)(b) "je výdajem cena, za kterou poplatník věc prokazatelně nabyl" (the price the taxpayer demonstrably paid; if acquired gratuitously, the value determined under the valuation act); no provision revalues assets on immigration, and the ZDP contains no "deemed acquisition" rule for new residents — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586 . The § 4 holding-period tests measure **ownership duration**, not residence, so pre-immigration ownership counts toward the 3/5/10-year tests (same URL). **No special rule for immigrants exists** (see UNVERIFIED list — no explicit Financial Administration guidance located).
- **Residence tests:** (i) **permanent home** ("bydliště" = a place in CZ where the taxpayer has a permanent dwelling under circumstances indicating intent to reside there permanently), or (ii) **habitual abode** = presence in CZ for **≥183 days** in a calendar year, consecutive or in several periods, each commenced day counting — ZDP § 2(4) — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586 ; PwC (also: a long-term visa alone does not create tax residence) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/residence . **Tie-breaker** for dual residence is the applicable double tax treaty (permanent home → centre of vital interests → habitual abode → nationality → mutual agreement) — PwC residence page and the Act's reference to international treaties (§ 2(3)) — same URLs.
- **NO digital-nomad or immigrant lump-sum tax regime (verified).** The Czech "Program digitální nomád" run by MPO is a **residence-permit facilitation programme only** for IT workers/freelancers who are citizens of Australia, Japan, Canada, Korea, New Zealand, the UK, the USA or Taiwan; its official FAQ answers the tax question by pointing to the ordinary ZDP residency rules and treaty tie-breaker, i.e. **no tax privilege** — https://mpo.gov.cz/assets/cz/zahranicni-obchod/ekonomicka-migrace/2023/12/FAQ-Program-DN.docx
- **Foreign dividends and interest for a new resident: taxed from day one** in the Czech return — either in the general base at 15%/23% with a foreign tax credit under the applicable treaty (ZDP § 38f) or in the elective 15% separate base (§ 16a) — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/foreign-tax-relief-and-tax-treaties . Interest from Czech deposit accounts/bonds paid to a Czech resident is subject to 15% final WHT (§ 36(2); PwC income-determination, same URL as § 3 above).

## 7. EXIT TAX ON LEAVING

- **NO general exit tax for individuals on emigration.** ZDP § 23g ("Zdanění při přemístění majetku bez změny vlastnictví") applies only to **corporate income taxpayers**: § 23g(2)(a)–(c) refer exclusively to "poplatníka daně z příjmů právnických osob"; § 6375 of the consolidated text likewise refers to legal persons ("Na žádost poplatníka daně z příjmů právnických osob … rozložení úhrady části daně … připadající na přemístění majetku bez změny vlastnictví") — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586
- **NO deemed disposal for individuals** holding shares/securities when they cease to be resident: the ZDP taxes individuals on actual realisation; there is no provision deeming a sale on departure (only § 23g for companies) — same URL.
- **Post-departure Czech taxation of certain items remains (source rules, not an exit charge):** § 22(1)(g)7 — "příjmy z prodeje investičních nástrojů … a majetkových práv registrovaných na území České republiky" and § 22(1)(h) — "příjmy z převodu podílů v obchodních korporacích, které mají sídlo na území České republiky" are Czech-source income of a non-resident **with no time limit in domestic law**; they are therefore taxable in CZ after emigration unless (i) a ZDP § 4 exemption applies (e.g. the 3-year securities / 5-year corporate-share time test, which measures ownership and thus survives emigration) or (ii) an applicable treaty allocates the gain exclusively to the new residence state — https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1992-586
- **The 5-year rule that does exist is a business-asset tail, not an emigration rule:** the time-test exemption does not apply to income from the transfer of a **corporate share acquired from the taxpayer's business property (obchodní majetek) within 5 years after termination of the self-employment activity** (§ 4(1)(u): "… pokud byl pořízen z obchodního majetku poplatníka, a to do 5 let po končení jeho činnosti…") and the equivalent tail for securities is **3 years** (§ 4(1)(w)) — same URL.
- **The flat tax / self-employed regime creates NO exit charge.** Leaving the flat-tax regime (voluntary exit by the 10th day of the following year, or loss of conditions notified within 15 days) simply triggers a return and settlement of tax and contributions for that year — https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/dane/dane/dan-z-prijmu/pausalni-dan/informace-k-institutu-pausalni-dane-pro-rok-2025
- EU/ATAD-compliant deferral for companies transferring assets within the EU (instalments up to 5 years) confirms the corporate-only design — consolidated ZDP text (same URL).
