# MOC - Career and Growth

Why the platform is being built beyond the platform itself, and how the work
converts into the next role.

## The strategy

Two tracks running together, explicitly:

- **Azure and Fabric for credibility and cashflow.** Enterprise-recognised,
 immediately legible, pays the bills.
- **The open-source K3s platform as differentiator and proof.** Nobody else
 applying for these roles has operated a data platform they built end to end.

The failure mode to avoid is tinkering indefinitely and shipping nothing. The
rule is: build a platform for one real use case, not a mini-Azure.

## Positioning

- Reframe identity as systems builder and platform architect, not dashboard
 developer.
- Target roles: Solutions Architect (Data and Integration), Platform Architect,
 Cloud Solution Architect (Data and AI), Product or Technical Product Architect,
 Founding Platform Engineer, Staff Engineer (Data Platform).
- Avoid as destinations: pure BI, governance and stewardship, dashboard
 factories, managing a team of analysts, requirements-gathering and reporting.

The sentence to reuse:

> I built a composable open-source data platform: Dagster + dbt + Postgres +
> FastAPI + Metabase + Streamlit + D3.js, containerised on K3s, deployed via
> Terraform, with RBAC, observability and API-level access control.

Every clause in that sentence is currently either unbuilt or partly built. It
becomes true in the order the [[Platform Delivery Plan]] lays out, which is the
point of tracking it here rather than in a CV draft.

## Study

The reading list, in order:

1. *Designing Data-Intensive Applications*, Kleppmann
2. *Platform Engineering on Kubernetes*, Salatino
3. *Fundamentals of Software Architecture*, Richards and Ford
4. *Terraform: Up and Running*, Brikman
5. *Software Architecture: The Hard Parts*, Ford, Richards et al

Certifications worth considering: AZ-305 (Azure Solutions Architect Expert) and
DP-600 (Fabric Analytics Engineer).

## Job signals to look for

End-to-end data solutions, API-first or event-driven design, platform treated as
a product, Kubernetes and containers with real CI/CD and IaC, and genuine
partnership with product and engineering.

Red flags: dashboard development as the core of the role, governance or catalogue
work as the core, or a mandate to manage analysts.
